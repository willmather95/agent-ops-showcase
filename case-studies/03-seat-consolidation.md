# Cutting the fleet down

By early October 2026 about 19 seats were working around one person. Scheduled routines had hit the platform usage limit on 2026-09-11, 09-14, 09-15 and again on 2026-10-05. Every extra seat was one more handoff with no owner.

## The rule I used

A team is 3 to 5 seats around one workflow. Each seat has one job. If two seats produce reports about the same property, nobody joins them, so they should be one seat with two modes.

## What changed (approved 2026-10-07)

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Separate scout and SEO seats for the same newsletter site | One SEO seat with a scout mode every other Wednesday |
| Separate writer, POV and X-post seats | One writer seat with site, newsletter, POV and X modes |
| A trust-and-hosting research seat with no ship path | Parked; its monthly brand check moved to the HSA researcher |
| A daily council refresh in the busiest usage window | Moved to weekly, mid-morning Wednesday |

After the map, the two teams run on 5 active seats between them: a shared lead, one researcher per property, a shared writer and a builder.

## How it was done safely

- A backup of every profile and routine was taken before any edit.
- Parked seats keep their instructions with a one-line park note on top and no routines. None was deleted, hidden or archived, and one message wakes any of them.
- Moved routines were created on the new seat first, then the old copy was paused (not deleted) so no job ran twice.
- Every changed routine gained one first line: read the team's mistakes file.
- The first weekly loop after the change was dry-run by hand and checked for the "read" line, a REVIEW line and a TESTS line in the log.

## What I took from it

Fewer seats with modes beat many seats with overlapping jobs. Parking instead of deleting made the change cheap to try and cheap to undo.
